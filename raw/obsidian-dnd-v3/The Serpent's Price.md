Published in 914 2E  by Theron Whitfod

The book details the events of [[476 2E - Seneth's Company]]. It is told coldly and emotionlessly, painting Seneth as a redeemable but ultimately wrathful person, and condemning the actions of the party, if not the individuals. Corrin is remarked upon as a coward but a good man, and Brother Zen a tragic, if groomed individual. However, Amanda is judged somewhat scavingly - the moral center of the party who looked the other way for a little too long.

It begins with the group, brought together intentionally via the Festival of Merit, a festival designed to create a group of adventurers' who could solve the countries problems. This immediately backfired, as whilst the group succeeded on the constructed dungeon, their first mission to liberate the town of Orlane lead to their capture at the hands of mercenaries. Orlane fell, but the group were able to escape and enact their revenge. Their mixed successes rewarded them with a favour, which they used to sway a local captain to take them to Nehal, to look for brighter horizons. 

It is theorised by Theron that the heist of the Nehalian Vault was performed by this group. Spearheaded by a greedy Seneth, and backed up to help their pirate captain, who, unbeknownst to the group but realised by historians later on, was actually Elven royalty. It is here that Seneth's role turns from leader into a more power-hungry figure. Regardless, upon their return it was discovered that the Tolgea Empire had taken the fall of Orlane as a sign of weakness, and had come to offer their 'support' in terms of a puppet mayor and Tolgean troops.

Aiming to support Limate, the group sat down to explain what happened at Orlane, and to justify the mistake made. They were able to convince Tolgea to back down, and were rewarded a plot of land as a reward. They were told afterwards that there were spies within their capital city, Khundhur, and something was afoot. Visiting the capital, it was believed that the party were currently helping Limate. 

However, Theron points out this may have been a coincidence, and that the group merely happened to go there. Which explains that after the warning and uncovering a Yuan-Ti plot to destroy the city, the group instead to support the Yuan-Ti instead of Khundur. Theron explains there are two narratives here, depending on how one looked at it. Firstly is the more cynical approach, the group let the Yuan-Ti flee the city, presumably to their keep and destroyed the city. However, there are a few discrepancies: firstly, released records from the Ambit indicate there was a significant amount of work in Khundhur, literally days before the explosion. Secondly, Corrin and Amanda, did not seem to be the kind who would accept this behaviour - first hand letters from Amanda have been retrieved from the (Postal Service Guild) seem to indicate a character of compassion and kindness - speaking unwell of violence. 

Around this time, Brother Zen died. It is believed this was the straw that broke the camels back, sending Seneth into opposition of the leaders of Limate (somewhat bizarrely) as well as the Tolgeans (Understandably), and thus lead the group, and the Yuan-Ti to what is now known as Halingo to construct his Ziggarat. It is here the group stopped splintering, and fractured. To give himself enough space, Seneth attacked the town of Heresy. Corrin, and Amanda took the town's side. A stalemate was reached after the husband of the town's mayor was burnt alive by Seneth's followers. An awkward stalemate. Corrin and Amanda abandoned the town, and Seneth, presumably unable to face off against their old leader and dear friend. 

Amanda returned to Limate where she faced a trial for abetting Seneth's crimes, but achieving a reduced punishment. She would perform her cleric duties for the Limate government for the rest of her life, after a stint in prison. Despite this, she was remembered fondly. She died and was buried in the keep just 20 years after leaving. Corrin vanished off the face of the earth. Tales were told in the local area of a grisly man popping out of the bushes to save them from mauraders or wild animals, but nothing ever stuck. No existence really exists of the man, only second hand accounts of witnesses and letters referencing his being. No one knows if he ever saw his face again. 

Seneth died, a hundred years later. Despite being surrounded by his kin, no one reported his death until several weeks after it had happened. He had some of Amanda's many letter's she had sent to him, each asking him to return to their keep and face his crimes - with her by his side. As far as we are aware, each one of them remained unanswered.

# Meta
Whilst in most areas this book remains circulated, in Halingo the work is banned, or at least heavily redacted considered a sacred tome. This is due to Seneth's resurrection. 

In taverns, the story is told is much more romantic, with Seneth as a doomed hero, destined to fail at saving his people and driven insane. In these versions, Corrin is often explained as a lover of Seneth, despite no documented proof.
