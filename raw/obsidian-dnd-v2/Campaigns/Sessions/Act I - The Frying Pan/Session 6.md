# Skipping Stones Wally DM
You wash up on the shoreline, bruised and battered. You hear scratching and growling against the walls. You have washed up on a small piece of land, with the water around you looking remarkably still. 

You see four totems buried into the hard rock at the bottom of the lake. They are arranged in a trapezoid fashion, with a large centre boulder pushed against the cliff wall behind it. 

The totem polwa with tiki-like designs extend about 5 feet above the water. The mouth of the tiki is atr water level giving each totem a menacing look. At the top of each pole, in the area of the forehead of the tikis is a ring of circles drawn in bullseye pattern.

The front left totem has 4 circles.
The back left toem has 6 circles.
The front right totem has 5 circles.
The back right totem has 8 circles.

To activate a totem a character must skip a rock into the mouth of the tiki face. However the stone must skip across the water a number of times equal to the rings drawn on the tikis foreheads.
Once the characters figure out that they need to skip a rock a number of times equal to the circles on the forehead of the totem, then we can shift to an ability type challenge. For lower level characters, I would recommend setting a DC of 10 (for the close ones) and a DC of 15 (for the further back ones) to see if a character can successfully skip a rock across the water and into the mouth with the correct amount of skips.

Subtley let them know that monsters will come out of the wall.

The room where the sarcophagus was found lies deep within the twisting veins of the mine, so far down that the walls are damp with moisture and encrusted with black, glistening minerals. The air is thick and stale, carrying a chill that seeps through armor and skin, as if it’s never known sunlight. The miners have long since abandoned this cavern; tools lay scattered, rusted and broken, as though dropped in a panic.

At the center of the cavern, a hollow shaft carved from the rock descends into the floor, ringed with jagged stones that seem almost deliberately placed. It’s here, within this pit, that the sarcophagus was unearthed. The ground surrounding it is dusted with strange, faintly phosphorescent dust that shimmers greenish in the low torchlight, lending an otherworldly glow to the chamber. The light casts long, unsettling shadows on the cavern walls, revealing faint carvings that twist and loop in nonsensical, spiralling patterns—a language or code that no miner could decipher.

The sarcophagus itself was wrenched from the earth with visible resistance; jagged rocks cling to its edges like claws, as though the land itself tried to hold it back. The stone where it lay is stained with dark streaks, an oily substance that no one dared touch, which seeps slowly from the pit’s edges. Any sound made here feels muffled, swallowed by the walls, and the silence feels as though it’s pressing in, almost alive.

The lanterns flicker inconsistently, dimming and flaring with an unnatural cadence, as if reacting to something unseen. If one stays in the room for too long, they might catch glimpses of something moving in the darkness—shadowy figures that hover just at the edge of sight, vanishing the moment one tries to look directly at them. Some claim these shadows resemble the forms of miners who vanished in recent weeks; others say they’re twisted into unnatural postures, as if they were pulled and stretched by forces beyond comprehension.

The room has a foreboding chill that clings to anyone who enters, and those who leave often find a strange, grayish dust lingering on their skin, which no amount of washing seems to remove. The miners whisper that this room is cursed, that the sarcophagus brought something with it from the depths—a presence that remains, watching, waiting. No one enters alone anymore, and even in groups, they keep their voices hushed, their breaths shallow, terrified of drawing the attention of whatever lies dormant beneath.
