## the tower

The **Unspoken Shield Hideout** stands as a grim, foreboding structure—recently built, yet showing rapid decay, as though time and corruption have already taken hold of it. The tower-like building is situated on a weather-beaten hill, its stone walls slick with moss and streaks of unnatural, dark stains seeping from cracks. Once meant to be a stronghold of the US, it now seems abandoned, its air heavy with the wild magic that lingers, twisting its foundations.

Outside, a large, battered **horse-drawn cart** sits tilted on its axles, the horse nowhere to be seen. The ground around it is churned with **deep tracks**—some lighter, from hurried footsteps, and others heavier, as if something weighed down the person or creature making them. The door to the hideout hangs slightly ajar, creaking in the wind, and a musty, unwelcoming odor seeps out. Vines, discolored and pulsing with faint traces of magical energy, climb the exterior walls like veins feeding the corruption within.

The first floor opens to a grim, sparsely furnished **war room**. At the center is a massive, dust-covered **table**, **a map of the region** spread across it, pinned down with rusting daggers at each corner. Locations of interest are crudely marked, with notes hastily scribbled in the margins. A **fireplace**, now cold and lined with soot, sits to one side, the smell of damp ash heavy in the air. **Fungus** grows in patches along the stone walls, especially in the corners where water drips from unseen cracks in the ceiling. The fungi pulses faintly with magic, casting eerie greenish light that flickers like dying embers.

A set of stone stairs, worn smooth from use, leads to the **second floor**, which houses the **living quarters**. There are **five small bedrooms**, their doors hanging crookedly from their hinges. The beds are unmade, and some are pushed to strange angles, as though someone left in a hurry. A few personal belongings—books, weapons, clothing—are strewn about, forgotten and covered in a thin layer of dust.

In the center of the second floor is a **grand dining table**, once intended for feasts but now used rarely, if ever. The table is lavishly carved, though grime and age have dulled its beauty. Scattered across it are **eating utensils**, left carelessly, some rusted from neglect. In the corner is a large **stove** for cooking, surrounded by half-burnt logs and broken cookware. Oddly, the air here smells faintly of something rotting, and there are signs of small animal infestation, or something worse.

The final floor is a disturbing blend of knowledge and chaos. Papers litter the ground, many stuck together with odd, sticky substances or singed by wild magic gone awry. This **research lab** holds countless **research notes**—some pinned haphazardly to the walls, others stacked in disorderly piles. Diagrams of **Mad Dog Morgan’s gang** and their activities are prominent, alongside notes on mysterious local occurrences tied to Wild Magic. The faint crackle of arcane energy lingers in the air, the remnants of experiments gone wrong.

At the far end of the floor, looming like an ominous sentinel, is what looks like a mechanical sarcophagus, a seemingly alien relic. The metal case—once sleek, perhaps even elegant in design—is now an aberration, twisted by the chaotic magic that pulses through it. **Tendrils of tarnished brass and copper** curl out from the pod’s centering on an embedded small black crystal, snaking across the stone floor like rusted veins, each vein alive with the corrosive thrum of wild magic. The very air around it seems to distort, as though the pod is a blister in the fabric of reality, a **scarred wound** upon the world of matter.

The pod’s surface is a nightmare of geometry, all angles and fractures where the arcane artifact has embedded itself—a jagged, obsidian shard **half-swallowed** by the pod’s structure. It juts out like a parasite, its dark surface etched with glyphs that **writhe and shift**, as though trying to escape their own meaning. **Vicious arcs of energy** leap sporadically from the shard, burning the air with the scent of scorched iron and sulphur, fizzling out before they reach the stone floor. The metal of the pod **buckles inward**, collapsing around the artifact, as though the object’s mere presence warps reality, drawing the pod toward an impossible center.

There is a dark, **oily translucence** to the glass covering the pod’s front—a membrane that seems less like a window and more like a layer of congealed shadow. Fleeting images ripple beneath its surface, glimpses of something—or someone—**entombed within**, their form obscured, twisted by the artifact’s influence. **Murmuring sounds** emanate from within, not quite words, but something older and more primal, echoing as if from the depths of the ocean, heard through layers of shifting water.

The artifact pulses. It sends out slow, creeping waves of magic, like **the heartbeat of some ancient leviathan**, barely stirring but waiting beneath. The once-fine machinery is now overrun with organic corruption—thin, **fibrous growths** crawl over the bolts and joints, creating a **bio-mechanical cancer** that breathes with an unnatural rhythm. The pod almost seems alive now, more than just an object—**a vessel fused with the wild magic** it was meant to contain.

The pod’s metallic surface is riddled with unnatural tendrils of wild magic, its once-gleaming finish now marred by strange glyphs that pulse with a dangerous light. Embedded within the pod is a **wild magic artifact**, its jagged edges poking out like a parasite leeching off the machine. The artifact’s corruption has clearly spread to the surrounding equipment, warping the walls and floor near it with wild, unpredictable energy. The pod is inactive but throbs with faint life, as though the artifact is keeping something within… waiting.

The hideout, once a place of refuge for the Unspoken Shield, now feels like a darkened, corrupted shell—a place where time, magic, and neglect have taken their toll.

# After
Cyndaquil, a Fire-type Pokémon, shares similarities with real-life animals, particularly echidnas and shrews.

1. **Echidna Influence**: Cyndaquil’s rounded body, small size, and pointed snout are reminiscent of the echidna, a spiny mammal found in Australia and New Guinea. Echidnas are known for curling up into a ball when threatened, a behavior that Cyndaquil mirrors in battle, as it can curl up and ignite its back with flames.

2. **Shrew-like Characteristics**: Cyndaquil’s timid, small stature, and burrowing tendencies are similar to those of shrews, small insectivorous mammals. Shrews have long, pointed noses and are known for being quick and elusive, much like Cyndaquil’s behavior of retreating when scared.

Random Encounters


**The Lockpick's Door**

There is a door in a smuggler's den. The door has a few archaic symbols at the top (This is a red herring. It's Theives Cant describing whatever's inside the room, you can be as subtle or as blatant as you like if you have a rogue) and what looks like seven locks with various writings on it. There is a thick iron turning handle in the centre of the door.

The first lock says "Open" in Common.

The second lock says "Close" in Elvish.

The third lock says "Open" in Dwarvish.

The fourth lock says "Close" in Infernal.

The fifth lock says "Open" in Giant.

The sixth lock says "Close" in Undercommon.

The objective of the puzzle is to unlock the locks which say "Open" (DC 10, or the trap goes off) and to leave the locks which say "Close" closed, and then turn the door handle, otherwise the Lightning Rune in the centre of the hallway (or another trap of your design) activates. The challenge comes from the character's language limitations. Sometimes they will have to do some guess-work.
