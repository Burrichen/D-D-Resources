The only stable structure in Perch is a great overhang
from a long-defunct sewer system, a grey stone lump
jutting out the side of Spire known as the Gutter.
When masonry falls from above, raiders on megacor-
vids rush the town, or errant skywhales scrape against
the city wall, the people of Perch rush and take cov-
er beneath it until danger passes. In less desperate
times, the workshops underneath the Gutter supply
the town (and much of the rest of Spire) with rope,
producing around a mile of cord each week.
Atop the Gutter is the Lady and Crow, an inn de-
signed to provide something approaching comfort to
travellers from outside Perch, with rooms of a regular
size and floors that are roughly at right-angles to the
Spire itself. The owner, an ashen-faced drow called
Skinner, takes care to import a small selection of
luxury goods for the occasional visitor with money
to spend.
