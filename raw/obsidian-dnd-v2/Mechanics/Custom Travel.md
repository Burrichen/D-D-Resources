# Player Rules
Journey rules will be used when moving to lairs/dungeons or going to an unusual place. If following a well-travelled and paved road (such as the Imperial Highway), there is little risk of getting lost, so a simplified version of the journey rules is applied – roles are still applied but the guide will automatically succeed (since they are following a road).
## Journey Roles:

| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Guide         | In charge of all decisions concerning route, rest.                          |
| Quartermaster | In charge of maintaining current supplies and finding new food in the wild. |
| Look-Out      | In charge of keeping watch.                                                 |
| Scout         | In charge of setting up camp and opening new trails                         |

There are four roles: **Guide**, **Scout**, **Quartermaster**, and **Lookout**. All four of these roles must be filled for the journey to start. If a party member takes on multiple roles, there is a penalty. Members who do not have an assignment may choose to provide the help action.

The **guide's** job is to navigate and lead the journey - they will have the final say on any decisions if one remains unmade and they decide the route or path to take (and will have to chart it on a map if there is one available). On an overland journey, the guide makes a check - on a successful check, the party travels "x" days* before they have an event. The higher the check, the more days they will progress without any problems.

If a check is passed, the party earns two progress points. If the DC is passed by more than five, the party earns three progress points. On a normal fail, there is one progress point given, but a fail by more than five results in zero progress points. In any case, there is always an event afterwards. Every journey has a difficulty level represented by the number of progress points that need to be achieved. Even simple journeys, like a dungeon, have a difficulty score (e.g. Blackrock Mine had a difficulty score of 2).

The **scout** keeps an eye out for shortcuts or potential danger.

The **quartermaster** keeps track of rations - on a long journey, this means food and water, but shorter journeys require keeping tabs on other forms of supply (e.g., like light sources if it is a dark area).

The **lookout** is also looking for potential danger.

The guide always rolls to attempt to make progress. After their roll, a secondary roll is made to determine which party member experiences the event - this may be a random encounter, a good encounter, a shortcut, etc.. They roll in turn to determine the outcome of said event. Success means avoiding problems - or even gaining a reward - while a failure can hinder the party.

Either way, the person who did the event loses stamina, which may manifest as levels of exhaustion at the end of the journey. After a journey ends, there is a constitution saving throw, the DC of which is affected by event successes or failures. Failing the saving throw means gaining a number of levels of exhaustion.
# Game Flow (Including DMing)
1. Design the map
2.  Generate hexes
3. Create random tables or use templates on the fly If your expecting a long journey, generating 6 'random' encounters is more than enough!
4. Play DnD
5. During the session, ask the characters how they want to traverse. Make them draw the exact line
6. Generate weather
7. Determine journey difficulty.
8. Use this website to determine the journey length: https://arcdream.com/tor/journeys/index.php
