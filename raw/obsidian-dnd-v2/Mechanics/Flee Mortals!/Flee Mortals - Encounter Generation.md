#FleeMortals #RandomEncounters #Encounters 
To give the characters a good but survivable challenge, fill each adventuring day with encounters that toal 6 to 8 points over the course of the day. 
E.g Two easy encounters (1 point each), One standard encounter (2 points) and one hard encounter (4 points) for a total of 8 points.

These budgets have been calibrassted so it's likely a party will run out of resources BUT survive the adventuring day. If you want a challenging adventuring day that doesn't ensure a parties survival, give yourself a budget of 12 points.

# Step 1: Choose Encounter Difficulty
Begin building your encounter by determining how difficult  you want the threat to be. This section provides guidance on choosing an encounter difficulty and how many daily encounter points each encounter is worth.

Though this section uses familiar language regarding encounter difficulty, the meanings of these terms don’t align perfectly with the core rules. For instance, for parties of 5th level and beyond, the core rules might call an encounter “hard,” but under our guidelines the encounter is standard or even easy. And some “deadly” encounters in the core rules are merely hard encounters under our guidelines.  These differences account for fewer encounters in a day.

## Trivial encounters (0 Points)

Trivial encounters require barely any effort on the part of the characters—like a group of 20th-level heroes taking on a handful of crawling claws or goblins. The characters are guaranteed to survive the fight and won’t spend many resources. These encounters can be fun to occasionally throw into your game, but for many groups, the novelty disappears quickly. Too many trivial encounters can feel like a waste of time.

A trivial encounter is worth 0 daily encounter points.
## Easy Encounters (1 Point)

Unless the characters have already depleted many of their resources and hit points, easy encounters won’t threaten their lives. Easy encounters are great for adventures that don’t give the characters much time to rest or for times when you want the heroes to feel like superheroes.
## Standard Encounters (2 Points)

Standard encounters are the most common for many adventuring groups. These battles deplete significant resources and hit points. While character death is uncommon in standard encounters, it isn’t impossible, especially if a player makes a poor tactical choice or just finds the dice are against them.

A standard encounter is worth 2 daily encounter points.
## Hard Encounters (4 Points)

Hard encounters are typically climactic encounters with bosses and their minions—or anything else that puts the characters’ lives in peril. Hard encounters are winnable, but the characters need all their resources, wits, and luck to survive.

A hard encounter is worth 4 daily encounter points.
## Extreme Encounters (8 Points)

Extreme encounters are ones where the characters will likely not survive if they try to fight to the bitter end. Such encounters rarely or never appear in most campaigns—though if the characters are very fit for them, they can typically survive (or come back to life after) such encounters.

An extreme encounter is worth 8 daily encounter points.
# Step 2: Determine CR Budget

Once you choose the desired difficulty of your encounter, you can determine its CR budget. To do so, you’ll need to know the average character level of the party (rounding down).

Using the Encounter CR per Character table, find the row that lists your party’s average character level, then find the column that lists your desired encounter difficulty. The number in that intersecting cell is your CR budget _per character_—just multiply that number by the number of characters in the party, and that’s your total CR budget to spend in the “Step 3: Spend CR Budget” section.

For example, if you wanted to build a hard encounter for a group of four 5th-level characters, you’d multiply 2½ (from the Encounter CR per Character table) by 4 (the number of characters) to get a total CR budget of 10.

## Companions and Retainers
For the purpose of encounter building, if the player characters adventure with one or more companions or retainers, consider them player characters but subtract 2 from their level.
## CR Cap
Some creatures who fit into your CR budget are too much for characters to handle. These monsters deal a lot of damage and create effects that are difficult o overcome for characters under a certain level. That's why the table provides a challenge rating cap for each average character's level.

## Solo Creatures
When using a solo creature, you shouldn't use the above encounter-building rules. Instead refer to the CR cap for the average character level, the depending on how difficult you want the encounter to be, select one solo creature within the CR range indicated below.

# Step 3: Spend CR Budget
Now you have your CR budget, spend it! Select creatures you want to use in the encounter and add their challenge ratings together until you get as close as you can to your CR budget. Here are some things to keep in mind:
- In a hard encounter - be careful you don't go over budget.
- In a easy encounter - be careful you don't go under budget.
- Remember when when encounter buildings, Minion's don't count as a full creature. Use a separate table. 
- You typically shouldn't use more than 3 non-minion creatures per character. For example, a party of four player characters usually shouldn't fight more than twelve non-minions at a time.
