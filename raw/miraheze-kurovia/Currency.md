# Currency

# Currency

Different regions of [[Kurovia]] use different systems of exchange. Some rely on minted or crafted currency, while others use barter, weighed goods or region-specific trade tokens.

## Currency by Region

- [[Ordon]], [[Skgurr]], [[Nareth]], [[Shinkira]] — [[Currency#Standard|Standard Coin]]
- [[Halingo]] — [[Currency#Ceramics|Ceramics]]
- [[Hjarthland]] — [[Currency#Bartering|Bartering]]
- [[Armada]] — [[Currency#PrizeMarks|Prize-Marks]]

## Standard

The **Standard** is the most formalised everyday currency in [[Ordon]], and by extension - the world. Even countries who have their own standard currency will often trade in the Standard to avoid regional complications.

Each piece is cut, stamped, lacquered and sometimes resin-sealed to make it resistant to moisture and wear.

The Standard is widely accepted in cities, industrial settlements and official trade.

### Common Denominations

- **Copper** — a low-value piece used for small purchases
- **Silver** — the basic everyday unit
- **Gold** — a high-value piece used for larger purchases and wages

### Regional Variants

Since metal dissolves in Skgurr, they instead use a metal base, with a wooden covering. The wood can be prised apart to view the metal inside. Stamps exist on the wood, rather than the metal base.

### Notes

Although the countries above use the Standard daily, pretty much every country use it for cross-country trading, especially when trades aren't that common.

## Ceramics

[[Halingo]] uses **ceramic coinage** rather than metal currency.

These pieces are fired from clay and glazed in distinctive colours. They are often stamped with dynastic, mercantile or regional symbols. Ceramic currency suits Halingo's desert environment, where clay is abundant and metal may be more valuable when used for tools, engines or weapons.

### Common Features

- Made from fired clay
- Often glazed, painted or stamped - the more detailed, the more valuable.
- Can be shaped as discs, squares or small tiles
- Resistant to heat and dry conditions
- Commonly used in [[Halingo]]

### Common Denominations

- **Shard** — a low-value piece
- **Tile** — the standard everyday unit
- **Sun** — a larger denomination used in trade and tribute

## Bartering

In [[Hjarthland]], exchange is often handled through **barter** rather than a formal national currency.

This is not purely fictional — it is based on real-world northern and frontier trade systems, such as those used in Norse, Icelandic, Arctic and sub-Arctic communities. In difficult climates, practical goods are often more valuable than abstract coinage.

### Common Trade Goods

Animal pelts and furs, Dried fish, Salt, Amber, Walrus ivory or carved bone, Wool and cloth, Livestock, Worked iron tools

### How It Works

Rather than using one coinage system, value is negotiated based on various factors such as scarcity, season, need, quality and local trust

Large deals may be measured in livestock, stores of food or trade goods, while everyday exchanges may rely on direct barter. For the largest deals, people may be used or their deeds may be sold with express permission.

### Notes

Many outposts still accept the [[Currency#Standard|Standard]], since they can use it for trading with Ordon. Some settlements may view this as an inconvenience and will devalue the currency. Others may need foreign supplies and will value it higher.

## Prize-Marks

In [[Armada]], currency is less standardised. Most ports accept foreign coinage, valuables and loot by weight, but the closest thing to a local currency is the **Prize-Mark**.

Prize-Marks are trade tokens made from shell, ivory, carved bone or hardwood, usually stamped by a captain, harbourmaster or local port authority. They often represent credit, plunder shares or dockside exchange rather than a fully centralised state currency. Generally prize-marks are only as good as their stamp - realistically the stamp could be on any material - and it's the seal that provides value.

### Common Features

- Made from shell, ivory, bone or hardwood
- Often irregular in shape
- Marked by ship, captain or port
- Used for taverns, supplies, docking fees and crew shares
- Commonly used in [[Armada]]

1.  1.  Common Terms

- **Bit** — a small everyday piece
- **Mark** — a common trade token
- **Share** — a higher-value token representing a portion of plunder or credit
