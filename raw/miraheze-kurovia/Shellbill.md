# Shellbill

**Shellbill**

A widespread semi-aquatic creature

![](/assets/miraheze-kurovia/Shellbill.png "A group of Shellbills")

A group of Shellbills

Adult and juvenile Shellbills. Individuals vary considerably in posture, shell pattern and plumage.

The **Shellbill** is a widespread semi-aquatic creature found throughout [[Ordon]], [[Skgurr]], [[Shinkira]] and [[Nareth]].

Recognisable by its duck-like head, broad paddling feet and heavy protective shell, the Shellbill is one of the most familiar freshwater animals across these regions.

They are generally harmless, social and curious creatures, although adults become remarkably stubborn when threatened.

## Appearance

Shellbills possess a body plan combining characteristics usually associated with waterfowl and shelled reptiles.

An adult typically possesses:

- a short, broad bill;
- smooth orange, yellow or brown plumage around the head and limbs;
- four powerful paddle-like feet;
- a large dark green or olive shell;
- and a prominent feathered tail extending beyond the rear of the shell.

The shell grows throughout the animal's life.

Young Shellbills possess relatively soft, brightly coloured shells which gradually darken and harden as they mature.

Older individuals may develop scratches, chips, algae and mineral staining across the shell.

**Identification note:**

Despite their appearance, Shellbills cannot fully withdraw into their shells.

They can, however, pull the head and limbs close enough to the body that the shell protects most vulnerable areas.

## Behaviour

Shellbills spend much of their lives in or around shallow water.

They are most commonly observed:

- paddling slowly across ponds and rivers;
- searching mud for food;
- resting together along banks;
- sleeping partially submerged;
- or following other Shellbills in loose family groups.

They are highly social.

Groups of between three and twelve individuals are common, although considerably larger gatherings may occur in areas with plentiful food.

Young Shellbills remain close to adults for several months after hatching.

Adults have been observed physically placing themselves between juveniles and perceived threats.

Despite their generally placid temperament, Shellbills can become extremely difficult to move once frightened.

A threatened individual usually lowers itself against the ground, pulls its limbs beneath the shell and simply refuses to move.

This behaviour has frustrated farmers, travellers and railway workers for generations.

## Diet

Shellbills are omnivorous.

Their diet consists primarily of:

- aquatic vegetation;
- seeds;
- grasses;
- insects;
- freshwater snails;
- small crustaceans;
- worms;
- fallen fruit;
- and occasional small fish.

They spend considerable periods with their bills beneath the surface, filtering soft sediment for edible material.

Shellbills living near human settlements quickly learn to associate people with food.

Feeding wild Shellbills is discouraged in several large cities because habituated animals have a tendency to occupy roads, docks and public gardens in large numbers.

## Shell

The shell is composed of overlapping hardened plates.

Colour varies between populations, but dark olive green is by far the most common.

Unlike ordinary protective shells, a Shellbill's carapace contains a thin layer of soft tissue beneath each plate.

Damaged plates can therefore slowly regrow.

This process may take several years.

The shell is highly resistant to biting predators and offers effective protection against falling debris and smaller projectiles.

It also makes the animal surprisingly heavy.

An adult Shellbill may appear small enough to lift until someone actually attempts to do so.

“Never pick up a Shellbill unless you're prepared to discover how much a pond weighs.”

— Ordon saying

## Swimming

Shellbills are considerably more agile in water than their appearance suggests.

Their broad feet provide powerful propulsion, while the shell naturally stabilises the body.

At normal speed they move slowly and smoothly.

When frightened, however, a Shellbill can produce a sudden burst of speed which frequently surprises observers.

They are capable of remaining underwater for several minutes.

Rather than actively swimming at depth, Shellbills commonly walk along riverbeds while searching for food.

Their shell provides enough weight to prevent them from floating uncontrollably toward the surface.

## Reproduction

Shellbills lay small clutches of thick-shelled eggs in nests dug into soft earth near water.

Both parents may remain near the nest.

After hatching, juvenile Shellbills instinctively seek water.

Their shells are initially flexible and require several months to fully harden.

Young Shellbills possess brighter yellow-orange plumage than most adults.

This colouration becomes progressively darker with age.

Family groups are particularly protective during this period.

## Distribution

Shellbills possess an unusually broad natural range.

Populations are established throughout:

- [[Ordon]];
- [[Skgurr]];
- [[Shinkira]];
- and [[Nareth]].

They favour rivers, lakes, marshes, canals, flooded farmland and sheltered coastal wetlands.

Their adaptability has allowed them to thrive in both wilderness and heavily populated environments.

Regional populations differ slightly in size, shell colour and behaviour.

## Ordon

Shellbills are a common sight around the canals, waterways and industrial reservoirs of [[Ordon]].

Urban populations have adapted exceptionally well to human activity.

They can frequently be found beneath bridges, beside locks and around public waterways.

Ordon Shellbills tend to possess particularly dark shells, likely due to generations of staining from soot and industrial sediment.

Workers traditionally regard a Shellbill sleeping near a factory entrance as good luck.

A Shellbill sitting directly **in** the entrance is regarded less favourably.

## Skgurr

[[Skgurr]] contains some of the largest known Shellbills.

Coastal populations inhabit sheltered inlets, estuaries and freshwater pools close to the sea.

Their shells are often thicker than those of inland populations.

During severe storms, Skgurr Shellbills gather in large groups behind rocks or within protected coastal pools.

They appear capable of detecting worsening weather several hours before conditions become visibly dangerous.

For this reason, some fishing communities historically watched local Shellbill behaviour when deciding whether to leave harbour.

## Shinkira

Shellbills are particularly common throughout the agricultural regions of [[Shinkira]].

They inhabit rice fields, irrigation channels, temple ponds and slow-moving rivers.

Unlike populations elsewhere, Shinkiran Shellbills are often tolerated directly within farmland because their feeding removes many insects and freshwater pests.

Several rural communities keep semi-domesticated populations.

These animals are not usually confined.

They simply learn where food is available and repeatedly return.

Small decorative sculptures of Shellbills are also common around ponds and gardens.

## Nareth

In [[Nareth]], Shellbills are most strongly associated with wetlands and broad river systems.

Local populations commonly travel considerable distances between seasonal feeding grounds.

Large migrations may involve hundreds of individuals moving downstream together.

Nareth Shellbills are usually somewhat lighter in colour than their counterparts elsewhere.

Whether this represents a distinct subspecies remains disputed.

## Relationship with Humans

Shellbills adapt remarkably well to human settlement.

They display little natural fear of people in areas where they are not hunted.

Some individuals become sufficiently accustomed to particular households or businesses that they return to the same location for years.

Domestication has occurred independently in several regions.

Domestic Shellbills are primarily kept as:

- garden animals;
- pest control;
- ornamental pond animals;
- companions;
- and occasionally sources of eggs.

They are rarely kept for meat.

Historically this was partly practical: killing an animal protected by a heavy shell requires considerably more effort than keeping conventional livestock.

Modern attitudes are generally more sentimental.

Shellbills have developed a reputation as peaceful, slightly foolish animals.

This reputation is not entirely deserved.

They possess excellent long-term spatial memory and can remember feeding locations for years.

## Temperament

Wild Shellbills are normally docile.

They may approach humans out of curiosity, particularly in areas where feeding is common.

Aggression is usually limited to:

- guarding nests;
- protecting juveniles;
- competition over food;
- or responding to attempts to physically move them.

A defensive Shellbill may hiss, snap its bill or strike with its forelimbs.

Their bite is painful but rarely dangerous.

The greatest risk generally comes from attempting to lift a frightened adult and dropping it.

## Predators

Young Shellbills are vulnerable to numerous predators.

Adults have considerably fewer natural enemies.

The combination of thick shell, aquatic mobility and group behaviour makes them difficult prey.

Large predators may still attack them, particularly by targeting the head or limbs.

When threatened as a group, Shellbills frequently cluster together with juveniles in the centre.

Individuals around the outside face outward.

The resulting formation has been compared to a pile of armoured ducks.

## Axius

Shellbills demonstrate no particularly unusual relationship with [[Axius]].

However, their shells absorb trace minerals from the surrounding environment throughout their lives.

In regions with naturally high Axius concentrations, old Shellbill shells may therefore display extremely weak Axius retention.

This effect is harmless.

Claims that carrying a discarded Shellbill plate provides protection from supernatural creatures are considered folklore.

This has not prevented such plates from being sold as charms.

## Regional Names

Due to their broad distribution, Shellbills possess numerous local names.

| Region       | Common Names         |
|--------------|----------------------|
| [[Ordon]]    | Shellbill, Canalback |
| [[Skgurr]]   | Stormshell, Baybill  |
| [[Shinkira]] | Shellbill, Pondback  |
| [[Nareth]]   | Riverback, Shellbill |

**Shellbill** has become the most widely used scholarly and international term.

## Notes

Shellbills are sometimes incorrectly described as hybrids.

There is no evidence that they originated through crossbreeding between existing animals.

Their combination of avian and reptilian characteristics represents their normal anatomy.

Fossil evidence suggests creatures recognisably similar to modern Shellbills have existed for a considerable period of time.

They are therefore considered a naturally occurring lineage rather than an artificially created species.
