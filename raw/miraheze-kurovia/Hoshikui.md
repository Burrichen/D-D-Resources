# Hoshikui

[[Hundred_Gods|Hundred Gods]] › [[Shinkira]] › Hoshikui

**Hoshikui**

One of the [[Hundred_Gods|Hundred Gods]]

The Star-Eater

![](/assets/miraheze-kurovia/Hoshikui.png "Hoshikui")

Hoshikui

A depiction of Hoshikui overlooking an isolated settlement in rural Shinkira.

**Hoshikui**, commonly translated as the **Star-Eater**, is one of the [[Hundred_Gods|Hundred Gods]] and an exceptionally rare [[Umbuso]] associated with the remote countryside of [[Shinkira]].

It is regarded by most modern Shinkirans as a creature of folklore. In villages where traditions surrounding Hoshikui have survived, however, stories concerning the creature are treated less as legends and more as instructions.

## Overview

Unlike more commonly encountered Umbuso, Hoshikui is not believed to represent a species. Every surviving account appears to describe the same entity.

Historical records describe an enormous black creature bearing vast membranous appendages and a trail of brilliant yellow lights stretching from its head along its spine and tail.

The origin and function of these lights remain unknown.

Hoshikui is listed among the [[Hundred_Gods|Hundred Gods]] because references to it appear in traditions considerably older than most surviving accounts of human resistance against the Umbuso.

Its apparent disappearance led scholars to classify the creature as **lost**.

Reports from rural Shinkira suggest that classification may be premature.

## Appearance

Hoshikui possesses an enormous and poorly defined body which absorbs almost all visible detail.

Its silhouette has been variously compared to a dragon, a malformed quadruped and an enormous mass of roots or hanging fabric.

Numerous tendril-like structures descend from its head, torso and limbs.

Two vast membranes rise from the upper body. They are usually referred to as wings, although no reliable account describes Hoshikui using them for flight.

The creature's defining characteristic is a chain of luminous yellow structures scattered across its head, spine and tail.

Some resemble tiny lights.

Others resemble four-pointed stars.

The largest appear as brilliant circular masses.

Despite their brightness, they seemingly cast almost no light upon Hoshikui itself.

The creature remains a silhouette even while surrounded by them.

## Behaviour

## Folklore

Rural Shinkiran folklore places particular importance upon the lights covering Hoshikui.

One tradition claims that each represents something consumed by the creature.

A small light represents a person.

A larger light represents a family.

A star represents an entire settlement.

No evidence exists to support this interpretation.

Another tradition claims the lights are stars removed from the night sky and carried permanently upon Hoshikui's body.

The creature's common name, **Star-Eater**, originates from this belief.

Children in several isolated Shinkiran communities are still taught a traditional warning:

  
*One light is nothing.*

&nbsp;

  
*Two lights are watching.*

&nbsp;

  
*Three lights know your name.*

&nbsp;

  
*If the whole sky walks, close the door.*

## Encounters

Almost every credible modern report originates from rural [[Shinkira]].

Sightings are particularly associated with terraced farmland, mountain valleys, forest roads and settlements containing shrines significantly older than the communities surrounding them.

Witnesses who remain inside buildings are substantially more likely to survive an encounter.

Individuals who attempt to approach the creature frequently disappear.

There are no confirmed records of Hoshikui being killed.

Several historical accounts describe attacks using conventional weapons or [[Axius]].

None record a successful injury.

**Encounter Guidance**

If Hoshikui is suspected to be nearby, remain within a building and do not attempt to observe it from close range.

Do not approach lights appearing in fields, forests or mountains.

Do not attempt to count the lights upon the creature.

Do not respond to unexplained voices outside the settlement.

Most importantly:

**Never follow the lights.**

## Notes

A recurring connection exists between Hoshikui sightings and extremely old rural shrines.

Many of these structures have no surviving religious affiliation.

Local communities continue maintaining them despite no longer knowing which god, spirit or ancestor they originally honoured.

Some scholars studying the [[Hundred_Gods|Hundred Gods]] have suggested these structures may never have been places of worship.

They may instead have been **boundary markers**.

Several surviving inscriptions associated with such shrines contain variations of the same phrase:

  
*Do not follow the lights.*

Whether these structures repel Hoshikui or simply mark locations where the creature has previously appeared remains unknown.
