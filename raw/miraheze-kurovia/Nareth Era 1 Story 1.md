# Nareth Era 1 Story 1

1.  The Last Flight of the Keeper

Ilyra steals the sleeping draught from Mother Edrin.

Lessa tastes it in the first mouthful.

“What is this?”

“Something to calm you.”

Lessa tries to stand. Her legs fail.

She understands before she reaches the floor.

“Ilyra.”

“I’m saving you.”

“No.”

It is the last word Lessa speaks before sleep takes her.

---

The reserve raft waits beneath Uru Taal.

It carries food for twenty-four people, seed, medicine, tools and the oldest surviving records of the river families.

Ilyra takes all of it.

Noma is waiting.

“Where are the others?” she asks.

“There is no time.”

“We agreed on twenty-four.”

“We agreed to survive.”

Noma looks at the sleeping girl in Ilyra’s arms.

Then she unties the raft.

The two women abandon everyone they claimed the reserve was meant to protect.

---

1.  1.  Teren

Teren finds them before they reach the outer barrier.

He drops from the walkway and lands hard on the raft.

“Take her back.”

Ilyra holds Lessa tighter.

“She chose to stay,” Teren says. “She chose to help them.”

“She doesn’t know what she is choosing.”

“She knows better than you.”

Behind them, the bells of Uru Taal begin to ring.

The silver growth has reached Low Stone.

People are waking.

Teren reaches for Lessa.

Noma strikes him with an oar.

He falls into the river.

For one moment, his hand remains hooked over the side of the raft.

Ilyra could reach him.

Then Lessa stirs in her arms.

Ilyra looks down.

Teren’s fingers slip away.

The current takes him beneath the gardens he once helped save.

Ilyra does not see him again.

---

1.  1.  What They Left Behind

The silver-root spreads through Low Stone before sunrise.

Without Lessa, no one can locate its deepest growth.

Without Ilyra, the food stores have no recognised authority.

Without Noma, the old river families turn upon one another.

For three days, Uru Taal nearly destroys itself.

It survives because the people Ilyra considered secondary take control.

Dema Oss organises the evacuation of Low Stone.

Mother Edrin burns the western gardens.

The garden workers open every food store and ration the supplies publicly.

Families from Aruven pull children from infected homes while old Uru Taal households shelter them in the trees.

A third of the settlement is lost.

Hundreds survive.

They survive without the Keeper.

That matters.

---

1.  The Sisters’ End

Lessa wakes two days downriver.

The raft has entered a narrow basin surrounded by black cliffs. Silver-leaf plants cover the banks.

Ilyra expects anger.

Lessa is quiet.

“You drugged me,” she says.

“I had to.”

“You stole me.”

“I saved you.”

“You saved my body.”

Lessa looks at the silver veins spreading along her hands.

“That is what the root does.”

Ilyra says nothing.

“It cuts away pain. Memory. Choice. Anything that threatens survival.”

Lessa meets her eyes.

“You became it.”

---

Lessa tries to return to Uru Taal.

Ilyra restrains her.

They struggle on the raft.

Lessa is stronger than she looks, but the draught has not left her body. She falls against the bank.

The silver plants bend towards her.

Roots emerge from the mud.

They wrap around her feet.

Ilyra hacks at them until her knife breaks.

Each severed root produces two more.

Lessa does not scream.

She sinks to her knees as pale growth spreads over her legs and into the stone beneath her.

“I could have fought it there,” she says.

“I won’t lose you.”

“You already did.”

By sunset, Lessa cannot move.

By morning, she is rooted to the basin.

She remains alive.

---

1.  1.  Noma’s Choice

Noma watches the roots spread across the cliffs.

“We cannot stay.”

Ilyra sits beside her sister.

“I’m not leaving her.”

“She is not your sister anymore.”

Ilyra turns on her.

Noma does not retreat.

“You wanted a backup plan. This is what remains of it.”

She takes seed, tools and as much food as one canoe can carry.

Before she leaves, she looks back at Lessa.

“Does she still understand us?”

Lessa answers herself.

“Yes.”

Noma departs alone.

Years later, she will claim that Ilyra murdered Teren, sacrificed Lessa and attempted to steal the reserve for herself.

She will leave out her own part.

That version becomes history in several settlements.

It is not entirely false.

---

1.  The Keeper and the Root

Ilyra remains in the basin.

She builds a shelter beside Lessa.

She brings water her sister does not need and food she cannot eat. She tells old stories, repeats forgotten jokes and sings the unfinished songs Lessa made as a child.

At first, Lessa answers.

Later, she speaks only during heavy rain.

Then only through the movement of leaves.

Years pass.

The silver growth spreads across the basin but travels no farther. Lessa holds it there.

She succeeds in containing the infection.

It costs her everything except consciousness.

Ilyra grows old beside her.

She never returns to Uru Taal.

Near the end of her life, she places one hand against Lessa’s silver bark.

“I kept you alive.”

The leaves move above her.

Lessa’s answer is almost too quiet to hear.

“I know.”

Not forgiveness.

Only acknowledgement.

Ilyra dies beneath the branches.

The roots take her body into the earth.

Lessa remains.

---

1.  The Legacy of Ilyra Venn

Ilyra was a gifted leader.

She prevented famine. She united two displaced peoples. She created reliable food stores, flood defences and emergency systems. Uru Taal became strong because of her.

She was also a disastrous leader.

She mistook responsibility for ownership. She treated survival as proof of righteousness. Every time fear demanded a sacrifice, she chose someone beyond the boundary of her love.

First strangers.

Then Aruven.

Then Uru Taal.

Finally Lessa herself.

Ilyra’s tragedy is not that she failed to save her sister.

It is that she never accepted that Lessa belonged to herself.

---

1.  What This Created for Nareth

&nbsp;

1.  1.  Uru Taal

The first major post-Oblate settlement in Nareth.

It grew from the union of the Stoneback survivors and the old river families. Its floating gardens, flood barriers and divided food stores become models copied across the rainforest.

After Ilyra’s betrayal, Uru Taal abolishes the office of Keeper.

No single person may control food, water and emergency labour again.

A council of workers, healers and district representatives replaces it.

1.  1.  Low Stone

Originally the district of the Stoneback refugees.

Its destruction becomes a symbol of both the silver-root disaster and the failure of hidden government.

Each year, Uru Taal leaves one ruined platform unrepaired in remembrance.

1.  1.  Aruven

A settlement harmed by Ilyra’s destruction of the water machine.

Its people carry a lasting hatred of Uru Taal and call Ilyra the River-Thief.

Aruven survivors later become influential advocates for shared control of rivers and irrigation.

1.  1.  The Silver-Root

A transformed organism that heals and feeds while removing pain, memory and independence.

It is not evil or intelligent in a human sense.

It preserves life by simplifying it.

Later Narethian healers will debate whether controlled use is possible.

1.  1.  The First Keeper

Ilyra creates Nareth’s earliest powerful central office.

Its success proves that organised authority can protect a settlement.

Its corruption creates a deep cultural suspicion of anyone who controls food or water.

Future Narethian governments will be shaped by both lessons.

1.  1.  The Hidden Reserve

The secret cache begins a long Narethian tradition of concealed survival stores.

Some communities treat them as responsible preparation.

Others consider hidden food a form of treason.

1.  1.  Teren

Remembered in Uru Taal as the man who held the garden line and later tried to stop the Keeper’s flight.

His body is never recovered.

The river workers adopt a saying:

- - Hold the line. Question the Keeper.\*\*

1.  1.  Noma

She survives and carries her version of the story elsewhere.

Her lies, half-truths and omissions ensure that Ilyra’s final days become disputed history.

Later historians will never agree on how much Noma planned.

1.  1.  Lessa, the Still Sister

Lessa becomes the first great mystery of Nareth’s history.

The basin where she remains rooted becomes known as the \*\*Pale Heart\*\*.

Some believe she guards the rainforest from the silver-root.

Some believe she is slowly becoming its new centre.

Pilgrims leave food she cannot eat and ask questions she rarely answers.

The oldest accounts insist that Lessa never died.

---

1.  The Meaning of Ilyra’s Story

This first story has established the central conflict of early Nareth:

- - How much of yourself may be sacrificed for survival before survival ceases to be worth protecting?\*\*

It has also created Nareth’s first:

- Major settlement
- Political office
- Refugee division
- Water conflict
- Public food system
- Government scandal
- Regional rivalry
- Historical traitor
- Folk hero
- Living sacred site
- Disputed national myth

Ilyra’s life is now complete.

Age One is not.

The next figures will inherit her settlement, her laws, her enemies, her lies and the sister she left rooted beneath the rainforest.
