> Once upon a time, a coward made a wish. "I want to be important."

**Ordon** is the largest and most influential country in Kurovia. It is known for its industrial cities, steam-powered machinery, airships and the workable form of Axius found beneath its land.

Ordon's Axius manifestation represents the concept of **progress**. It can be refined into fuel, conductive machinery, armour and substances that increase human productivity. Although the country's capital possesses advanced technology, the settlements beyond its industrial centre increasingly resemble older and more traditional societies, deprived of the progress that so defines Central Ordon.

## Overview

Ordon is a country divided between rapid industrial development and older rural traditions. Its central cities are filled with factories, railways, workshops and Axius-powered technology, while its distant provinces remain dependent on agriculture, traditional crafts and local customs.

It is governed by an **Oligarchy of Proctors**, seated in the capital, [Morgenwelde](/content/locations/morgenwelde.md). Axius is tolerated.

## Geography

A gloomy land of mist-shrouded mountains, steep gorges and dark forests.

### Regions and provinces

- **Heartlands**: the industrial and political heart of the country.
- **Northern Ordon**: the colder, harsher and more independent portion of Ordon.
- **Lowlands**: large peasantlands, enjoying gentle weather and an easy climate.
- **Eastern Ordon**: primarily known for its transport city.
- **Western Ordon**: the "Frontier" of Ordon; many upstarts are found here.

### Climate

Ordon's climate is much like the real world, with strong seasonal effects. The summers are hot, the winters freezing.

Industrialised areas have light pollution, and the glow of the Barrier around the Eastern Area keeps many up at night.

### Natural resources

Ordon's most important natural resource is its regional form of Axius, **Morrow**, a malleable ore with different properties depending on how it is processed. Other resources include coal, iron, timber, agricultural land, stone and navigable rivers.

## Population

Ordon contains approximately **40%** of Kurovia's total population. Its inhabitants are unevenly distributed, with large numbers living in the capital and other industrial centres.

## Economy

Ordon possesses one of the most developed economies in Kurovia. Its wealth is closely connected to Axius mining, manufacturing and the production of advanced machinery.

Major industries:

- Axius mining and refinement
- Steam machinery
- Airship construction
- Armour and weapons
- Rail transport
- Pharmaceuticals and productivity drugs
- Agriculture
- Traditional rural crafts

## Axius in Ordon

Ordon's manifestation of Axius represents **progress**. It appears as a malleable ore that can be treated in several different ways.

### Fuel

When condensed, the ore becomes an energy source more powerful than coal. Much like coal, it also belches out fumes, at a much higher rate. The resulting energy is connected to the Weave.

### Conductive material

When treated lightly, Axius becomes highly conductive. This property has allowed Ordonian engineers to develop complex steam machinery and other advanced technologies.

### Armour

Ordon's strongest armour is manufactured from Axius ore. The armour provides exceptional protection, but prolonged use can create an attachment between the wearer and the metal.

Some wearers eventually become unable to remove it. This effect is not widely known among the public.

### Productivity drugs

Axius can be refined into a substance that increases concentration, energy and productivity.

## Technology

Ordon is the most technologically developed region of Kurovia, although this technology is not distributed evenly. The capital represents the height of Ordonian technology. Technology becomes less common further from the capital: smaller towns may possess rail connections and basic machinery, while remote villages continue to depend on traditional tools, animals and magic.

## History

Quinten Hearspar always wanted to be a scholar. Defeat crept into his becoming, his most familiar feeling. He found himself toiling in the fields, just like his father before him. His blood, sweat and body being given to the ground, for an owner who never even showed his face at their plot, but would still demand ever-increasing payments.

Quinten's luck wouldn't end there, after a bandit-raid led to his mother being left immobile and brain damaged. Five months later, his father abandoned them, going on to raise a family with another woman. A year after that, he stopped supporting them financially, while mother and son burned through their collective savings to rehabilitate her.

One hot day, Quinten suffered a stroke whilst working in the field. His body spasmed, falling to the ground, choking on his own spit. His fractured mind glimpsed something no other mortal had. A world of pure order, of complete logic, lacking in all the wrongs this world had shown him.

He awoke days later in that field, his body deemed unworthy of a coffin. Quinten could never see the world in the same way again. Lines of logic, as clear as words on parchment, began appearing to him. He tried to tell those who asked about his recovery, but the stroke made his speech slurred, and when he was speaking coherently, the content of his speeches made very little sense. When he followed the logic, good things began to happen. He began making real money by implementing new irrigation techniques on his plot, which earned him a promotion. His mother was left alone, funds pulled when his new brain couldn't understand the familial bond.

Using his acquired funds, he left his farm. He travelled day and night, refusing to eat, drink or sleep, until he found an old quarry. The lines of logic showed him where to go: a golden nugget, in the middle of a large pile of rocks, unseeable by the naked eye. Painfully, severing his vocal cords, he ate the ore.

Like a man possessed, he dug. By the time his body gave out, he had collected another five of these ores, his body wrapped around them like a nursing mother. The first he sold, and using those funds, gathered a group of hearty workers to dig the area. With those profits, he secured the land itself. Then, he stopped selling. Creating blueprints and schemes he saw in his feverish dreams the first night he imbibed the ore, he made the first rudimentary steam engine. He used this for nothing other than drilling.

One day, the lines began to fade. It wasn't long after that when the losses began. In a desperate bid to see what he once did, he sank money into the last golden lines of order: into a machine to show him the logic of the world, to show him that clockwork universe. **Eye of Gold** was its name. By utilising its power, Quinten was able to transform his wealth into a settlement, then a town, then a city. Eventually it became the capital, [Morgenwelde](/content/locations/morgenwelde.md).

Technological advancements filled the city, and it became an industrial powerhouse, at the cost of those living there. The use of Morrow became widespread. Quinten found his body growing old, less useful. The logic told him to discard it. His body was found, mutilated and partially fused to the engine. The engine, if it ever worked, never will again.

## Foreign relations

### [Halingo](/content/locations/halingo.md)

Halingo and Ordon stand at a tense but neutral standoff. The rail network connecting the two countries was financed by both of them, a sign of co-operation, an outward truce. The truth of it is both countries are aware they both fill a niche: the Ordon ore and Ichor generate power. The leaders of Ordon are too self-centred and the Diesel Barons are too greedy to ever work together; both are aware only one can truly survive. Both vie for Shinkira's affection, a stalemate in the game they play. For now though, to the common folk, it appears as if both are in a truce.

### Skgurr

For several hundred years after the Oblate, the area now known as **Skgurr** was a violent, desolate wasteland, even if it did have inhabitants. The ancient order of the Ambit (political allies of Ordon) was primarily responsible for turning Skgurr into a habitable area. Locals were begrudgingly accepting. It was when Ordon believed it was entitled to the mysterious magical items that washed up along Skgurr's shoreline that things got heated.

A war machine was deployed to scare the locals and rescue the troops. When this machine began eroding due to the Acid Rain, a group of Steelbacks rescued the crew inside. This one act of heroism turned a simmering feud into a tentative alliance, the deployed platoon paying off their debt by joining the Steelbacks themselves, but not before returning to Ordon with some minor magical items as a delegacy. Knowing Skgurr's weather will forever hold off Ordon's war machines, the two countries became unlikely allies, with Ordon supplying basic goods in return for magical items.

### Shinkira

For hundreds of years, Shinkira was unassailable. Inhabited by [Umbuso](/content/lore/umbuso.md), it was believed no humans could live on that island. It was only when an Ordonian airship flew over the island patrolling for Armada that they realised the towering creatures that once defined the island were now gone. Instead, villages had sprung up. People were there all along.

Ordon attempted to deploy a delegacy as soon as possible, but news of this new country was leaked by an informant. The bureaucracy of Ordon had slowed it down, whilst other countries acted instinctively. In an act of cruelty, Halingo representatives reached the island on an oil-powered vessel and, knowing they had no chance of winning over the noble people, spent their time besmirching Ordon's name: not lying, but feeding them the worst stories of Ordon's sordid past. By the time the delegacy arrived, they were treated as hostile.

Instead, Shinkira decided to be independent, increasingly aware that if they were to ally firmly with any greater power, that power would instantly revolt against the other. Thus Shinkira remains politically neutral, but accepting of all, for the greater good. It is not known how long this neutrality will last.

### Nareth

Nareth and Ordon have a simple relationship. Nareth will back up Ordon when the time comes (the Nareth Truce); in return, Ordon will not interfere in Nareth affairs, and supplies them with 23 children each calendar year.

### Bratislor

It is safe to assume that without the Barrier, the two would be at violent war with one another. But, given the choice, Bratislor would be at war with everyone. The great Barrier divides the two countries, and ensures Bratislor is contained. That isn't to say the Barrier is impenetrable, far from it. Scholars and architects from both Ordon and Hjarthland worked in tandem to construct this Barrier, but it is Ordon that powers it and keeps it from functioning.

It is unclear whether it is the lack of power or a deliberate, sinister implication that the further north you travel, the weaker the Barrier becomes. It isn't unheard of for the occasional monstrosity, vaguely recognisable as human, to pass through. Most are destroyed instantly.

There have been no attempts from inside the Barrier to communicate formally.

### Armada

No real relationship exists between these two. Trusting reports from Skgurr, if Armada were ever to get close to the shoreline, it would be met with hostility.
