**Mournbacks** are enormous predatory Abominations distinguished by elongated limbs, exposed growths of bone and tendon, and extreme asymmetry between different parts of the body.

They are rare, highly territorial, and exceptionally violent once disturbed.

Despite their appearance, Mournbacks are living animals rather than [Umbuso](/content/lore/umbuso.md), and confirmed specimens have been successfully killed.

**DANGER — CR 12**

A Mournback should never be approached by an unprepared travelling party.

Its reach, speed and capacity to continue fighting after catastrophic injury make close engagement particularly dangerous.

## Appearance

Adult Mournbacks stand significantly taller than a human when fully upright.

Their anatomy varies between individuals, but common characteristics include:

- an emaciated torso;
- disproportionately long forelimbs;
- one limb frequently developing far larger than the other;
- exposed bone, tendon and hardened growths;
- thick mats of coarse hair;
- elongated claws;
- and horn-like or branch-like growth around the head and shoulders.

The oversized arm is particularly dangerous.

Mournbacks use it both for locomotion and as a striking limb capable of overturning carts, breaking doors and throwing adult humans considerable distances.

**Abomination variance**

No two Mournbacks are anatomically identical.

The species is identified by recurring skeletal and behavioural traits rather than exact appearance.

## Behaviour

Mournbacks are solitary and strongly territorial.

Most spend daylight hours concealed within ruins, caves, abandoned structures or dense woodland.

They become considerably more active at dusk and during poor weather.

Once threatened, a Mournback displays little concern for self-preservation.

Individuals have continued attacking after:

- loss of an eye;
- broken limbs;
- deep abdominal injuries;
- extensive blood loss;
- and partial severing of smaller extremities.

This behaviour has led to the mistaken belief that they cannot feel pain.

Dissection suggests otherwise.

They simply do not reliably respond to it.

## Hunting

Mournbacks are opportunistic predators.

They favour large prey and appear particularly sensitive to sudden movement and loud noise.

Their usual attack begins with a rapid charge followed by sweeping strikes from the enlarged forelimb.

At close range they bite, claw and attempt to pin prey beneath their body.

They are capable climbers despite their size, though their unusual proportions make descending considerably less graceful.

## Origin

The origin of Mournbacks remains uncertain.

Their anatomy is too inconsistent for them to represent a conventional species without significant biological abnormality.

Most scholars classify them as true **Abominations** — organisms whose development has been radically altered through mutation, environmental influence, Axius, or another poorly understood process.

Whether Mournbacks reproduce normally has never been confirmed.

Juvenile specimens are unknown.

**FIELD BIOLOGY NOTE**

Every recovered Mournback has been physically mature.

No nest has been identified.

No egg has been identified.

No pregnant specimen has been identified.

This does not mean that they appear from nowhere.

It means we do not currently know where they begin.

## Habitat

Mournbacks favour isolated locations capable of sheltering their considerable size.

Common territories include:

- abandoned settlements;
- ruined religious structures;
- forested valleys;
- cave systems;
- derelict estates;
- and old fortifications.

Their territories are usually recognisable through broken vegetation, deep claw marks and partially consumed carcasses.

## Relationship with People

Mournbacks do not ordinarily seek populated settlements.

Most attacks occur when travellers, hunters or scavengers unknowingly enter established territory.

Once a Mournback associates a settlement with food or repeated disturbance, removal becomes necessary.

Professional monster hunters occasionally accept such contracts.

Casual attempts to kill one are responsible for a disproportionate number of Mournback-related deaths.

## Combat Assessment

**Threat: Extreme**

Mournbacks combine exceptional reach, physical strength and durability with unpredictable movement.

They are particularly dangerous in confined environments where their sweeping attacks can control large areas.

Experienced hunters favour open ground, ranged attacks and repeated injury to the legs.

## Known Weaknesses

Mournbacks are difficult to kill, but not unusually resistant to conventional injury.

Effective approaches include:

- crippling the legs before closing distance;
- attacking from several directions;
- maintaining significant separation from the enlarged arm;
- exploiting their tendency to pursue the nearest moving target;
- and sustained blood loss.

Fire can frighten some individuals but should not be considered reliable.

## Axius

Mournback tissue frequently contains elevated but highly irregular concentrations of Axius.

Whether Axius causes their abnormal development or simply accumulates within already altered tissue remains disputed.

Attempts to deliberately reproduce Mournback-like mutations have not produced viable organisms.

## Adventure Uses

A Mournback may serve as:

- a dangerous creature occupying abandoned ruins;
- the cause of missing travellers;
- a monster-hunting contract;
- an Abomination whose corpse is valuable to researchers;
- evidence of contamination affecting local wildlife;
- or a creature driven from its old territory by something considerably worse.

> A dead Mournback is dangerous only in the usual ways. Make certain it is dead first.
