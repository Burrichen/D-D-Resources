**ARCHIVAL NOTICE**

**Umbuso** and **the Hundred Gods** describe the same class of entity.

*Umbuso* is the modern scholarly term.

*Hundred Gods* is the traditional Shinkiran designation.

The name is not evidence that exactly one hundred entities exist.

Catalogue numbers between **UMB-001** and **UMB-100** are administrative identifiers only.

Missing numbers should not be interpreted as unused.

“They called them gods because nothing smaller could explain them.”

— Author unknown

## Classification

The **Umbuso** are an irregular group of extremely rare creatures documented throughout the oldest surviving records of Kurovia.

They are not believed to form a single species.

No shared anatomy, ancestry, habitat or reproductive system has been established.

Classification instead reflects a recurring collection of characteristics:

- extreme biological abnormality;
- extraordinary longevity or historical continuity;
- unusually high destructive potential;
- resistance to conventional intervention;
- and behaviour which cannot be adequately explained by existing natural sciences.

Most people will never encounter an Umbuso.

This is considered desirable.

## The Hundred Gods

The oldest substantial records originate in Shinkira, where such entities were collectively referred to as the **Hundred Gods**.

The translation is imperfect.

Some were worshipped.

Others were avoided.

Several appear to have inspired buildings, roads, rituals and entire settlements designed around the simple objective of never attracting their attention.

## Hazard Assessment

Confirmed Umbuso receive an **[UHI](/content/lore/umbuso-hazard-index.md)** assessment.

Six factors are rated from **1 to 5**:

**S** — Scale\
**L** — Lethality\
**A** — Activation\
**C** — Containability\
**P** — Predictability\
**K** — Knowledge

**1 represents maximum hazard.**

**5 represents minimum hazard.**

The six values are averaged to produce the final UHI.

**EXAMPLE**

UMB-017 // HOSHIKUI

UHI **2.50-III**

S3 / L2 / A3 / C2 / P3 / K2

See: **[Umbuso Hazard Index](/content/lore/umbuso-hazard-index.md)**

## Disappearance

Historical records suggest Umbuso were once substantially more common.

Why they became rare is unknown.

Extinction has been proposed.

So has migration.

Dormancy.

Changes in Axius.

Human expansion.

None satisfactorily explains the available evidence.

The Umbuso may not have disappeared.

Humanity may simply have stopped finding them.
