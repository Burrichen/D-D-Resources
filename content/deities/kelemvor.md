## Overview

Kelemvor is one of the few death gods who believes that death should be honoured, not used as a tool. He acts as the judge of the damned, sending those who have passed on to their appropriate final resting place. He is an Old God, but willing to swap sides to gain more power. Many of the other gods "don't get them". Each Kelemvor tends to associate with different gods, but the most common company is [Hermod](/content/deities/hermod.md) and [Chauntea](/content/deities/chauntea.md).

Kelemvor is less a god than a title. The role passes to deserving mortals when they die, and the new Death is often groomed before taking its place. Some mortals don't know this, but a god of death who never died doesn't make any sense. The first Kelemvors are lost to time; five are known.

## Motivation and philosophy

Kelemvor's motivation is very simple. They believe death should be an honoured tradition, and despise it when other gods use it for personal gain or for their beliefs. They do not willingly socialise with others.

## The holders of the title

### Abernath: the Grasping Passage

Abernath believed he was above impunity. He was the first being ever to die in the world, a bitter old man, and was forced into the role of Kelemvor, although the term hadn't been coined yet. Cold and calculating, he prided himself on his self-taught merit. As the forefounder of all the Dead Gods, he was woefully mishandled, bitter and ineffective, and he left no lasting legacy.

Because no ceremony for transferring the power had been created, there was no handover. When his estranged daughter Dianye died, her soul was held in [Carceri](/content/locations/carceri.md) until Abernath relinquished the position, and she then had to take on his workload. Only when Dianye was finally able to control her newfound godhood could the Cognitive Age begin, ending the Grasping Passage.

### Dianye: 0 CA

Dianye lived a very normal life. There was nothing special about her, apart from the fact that her father was Death; he had abandoned her while he was still mortal. On her ascension, instead of a peaceful afterlife, she found a whole new series of struggles had followed her. She worked out what had happened to her by pulling her father back to life and speaking with him directly. Breathing life back into him was a sacrilegious act. Abernath was of no use: Dianye asked for help, he refused to support her, and the two bickered often. Disgusted but determined, Dianye realised she was trapped for the rest of eternity.

Her powers didn't come close to Abernath's, and she realised she had her work cut out for her. Carving out her own space inside her own realm, she created an institution to work hard. It took thousands of years to create a sustainable environment where she could work, but she brought the Grasping Passage to an end. She is depicted as the very first aasimar.

In a cruel twist of fate, just as Dianye was beginning to feel comfortable in her position, she began ageing, her powers dwindling and her mind fading. She spent her last few hundred years recording all she knew, and set about transferring her power to a mortal she believed was worthy. It was she who created the title Kelemvor.

### Carvell: 6,924 CA

Carvell was a man of the law. Inspired by his father, he went out to make a difference in the world. Within his first year of service he was killed protecting a family from outlaws, and his own father held his body as he died. Dianye saw the purity of his heart, and knew this tragedy could still have a somewhat happier ending.

Carvell was taken and given full power. The transfer was simple and easy, and Carvell worked to reinforce and build on what Dianye had founded. When his father's time came, instead of bringing him to death, Carvell employed him to help with his duties, free to retire at any point. But the years took their toll on Carvell, and he felt guilt for those he transported. So the seed was sown for the next Kelemvor.

### Wernicke: the Lingering Silence

Wernicke was an executioner of the Cognitive Age who died by mistake. Carvell chose him for his clear-headedness while performing executions, but after the transfer the two famously hated each other. They had completely different views of the role: Carvell wanted it to be an honourable, neutral and homely experience, while Wernicke wanted it efficient but cold. They argued often, and tensions ran high as Carvell knew his time was up. As a kind of revenge, Wernicke ushered in the Lingering Silence, showing how bad he could make the world if he chose.

Eventually Wernicke realised how spiteful he had been. He tried to resurrect his master so he could redo his studies and learn how to be a good Kelemvor, but instead he was met by Dianye. She comforted him and taught him anew. They worked together for many years, and Wernicke ended up serving a very long time, proud of how far he had come.

Then the Oblate happened, and for the first time in centuries Kelemvor's powers were out of their hands. The sheer amount of death strained their powers, and they quickly lost control. Seeing how the world that had been so well maintained had fallen, and how he had let down everyone (Carvell, Dianye), Wernicke killed himself.

## Worship

- **Mourcroft** (Maillon, Thevethalas) grew up around a gravesite and became one of the main sites of Kelemvor worship. Locals say its mist is a blessing from Kelemvor, marking it as one of the places closest to the realm of Death. Its High Lord, Cenric Mourbraith, comes from a long line of death-priests and holds strong influence over the Priesthood of Kelemvor.
